# What Is Dyslipidemia?

Dyslipidemia refers to unhealthy levels of cholesterol or triglycerides in the blood. High LDL ("bad" cholesterol) and elevated triglycerides can increase the risk of heart disease, stroke, and other cardiovascular complications.

Making healthier food choices and avoiding certain foods can play an important role in maintaining healthy cholesterol levels.

## Foods to Avoid

### 🍔 Fried Foods

Avoid foods such as:

- Fried chicken
- French fries
- Pakoras
- Chips

These foods are often high in unhealthy fats and calories.

### 🧈 Foods High in Saturated Fat

Reduce consumption of:

- Butter
- Excess ghee
- Cream
- Full-fat cheese
- Fatty cuts of meat

Excess saturated fat may contribute to increased LDL cholesterol levels.

### 🍰 Sugary Foods

Limit or avoid:

- Cakes
- Cookies
- Ice cream
- Chocolates
- Sweet desserts

High sugar intake can negatively affect cholesterol and triglyceride levels.

### 🥤 Sugary Beverages

Reduce consumption of:

- Cola drinks
- Soft drinks
- Sweetened fruit juices
- Milkshakes

These beverages can contribute to weight gain and metabolic health concerns.

### 🍖 Processed Meats

Examples include:

- Bacon
- Sausages
- Salami
- Hot dogs

Processed meats are often high in saturated fats and sodium.

### 🍕 Fast Foods

Avoid or limit:

- Pizza
- Burgers
- Fried snacks
- Instant foods

These foods frequently contain unhealthy fats, excess sodium, and refined carbohydrates.

## Foods That May Help Improve Cholesterol Levels

Choose healthier options such as:

- Oats
- Barley
- Beans
- Lentils
- Fish (especially salmon and sardines)
- Almonds
- Walnuts
- Olive oil
- Fruits
- Vegetables

## Lifestyle Changes for Better Cholesterol Management

- Exercise for at least 150 minutes per week.
- Maintain a healthy weight.
- Stop smoking.
- Limit alcohol consumption.
- Get adequate sleep.
- Follow a balanced diet rich in fiber and healthy fats.
